# Researching Leadership Accountability

## Core rule

Record evidence-rich events, not conclusions about a person's overall worth or ideology. Separate what happened, responsibility, documented concern, outcome, response and legal status.

Read `leadership-accountability/LIBRARY_CONFIG.md` before writing.

## Workflow

1. Discover candidates from primary statements, government/Knesset records, State Comptroller/inquiry material, courts, police/legal records, reputable Israeli/international reporting and strong research evidence.
2. Resolve the exact event/statement/decision and the subject's role/authority at that time.
3. Choose the narrowest category from the canonical taxonomy.
4. For false statements, isolate a checkable factual assertion and use fact-check discipline.
5. For failures, require a documented responsibility link; a bad outcome alone is not enough.
6. For conflict/political self-interest, require specific evidence of the conflict/incentive/tension. Do not infer motive from disagreement.
7. For legal/ethical matters, preserve exact procedural status and later reversals/acquittals/dismissals.
8. Capture the subject's material response/defense and credible contrary evidence.
9. Deduplicate multiple reports of the same event.
10. Social posts may establish the subject's own statement/media but cannot alone prove contested failures, motives or guilt.
11. Write canonical Event + Sources before media publication.

## Completion checks

- subject and role at time are correct;
- category is evidence-matched;
- responsibility link is explicit for failure categories;
- legal status is exact when applicable;
- response/defense recorded when material;
- interpretation does not outrun evidence;
- source/media records link to one canonical event.
